on the workbenchon the workbench

Seashell is a TU Delft start-up project by Pau Adell and William Roskaer. It began as a 42 mm hub in a prototyping course; four versions later it is a 30 mm shell, each checked by script.

the shell, opened

a cellular modem in a 30 mm shella cellular modem in a 30 mm shell

Nordic's nRF9151, a 12 × 11 mm modem with GNSS, shares a 26 mm board with the hidden dots.

The modem joins LTE-M, a mobile network built for small devices, through a 2.5 mm eSIM chip. Calls run over the internet.

A small antenna under the top finds where you are, so the help call to the contact you chose carries your position.

A second Nordic chip runs LE Audio for earbuds on land and loads your music over USB in the charging cradle.

165 hidden dots

White LEDs at 0.8 mm under a skin the colour of the shell. Off, you see one plain colour; lit, a name in two lines of four.

one colour, all overone colour, all over

Body and top are one colour. The dots shine through a skin of that same colour, so the shell looks plain until someone calls.

The shape is a pebble: 30 mm across and 8 mm at the crown, round so the whole top can turn.

The names read indoors, at dusk and in shade. In full sun, cup a hand over the shell.

Berry is the first colour. Others follow only if their glow reads well enough through the skin.

a cable for the watera cable for the water

A few centimetres of sea stop a radio signal, which is why nothing reaches the shell under the surface. So in the water your headset plugs into the cradle and the sound runs through a cable. On land the shell pairs with LE Audio earbuds. Older earbuds without LE Audio will not connect.
