Yes. The shell has no microphone or speaker. In the water a wired headset plugs into the cradle; on land it pairs with LE Audio earbuds.
Earbuds with Bluetooth LE Audio. Older earbuds that only use classic Bluetooth will not connect, because the shell's Bluetooth chip (Nordic nRF5340) has LE Audio only.
Their short name lights up in the shell, up to four letters a line: LOU, MUM. It reads indoors, at dusk and in shade. In full sun, cup a hand over it.

Hold the top for three seconds. A ring of dots fills in the shell, then it shows HELP / CALL and rings the contact you chose, with your position.
The contact you chose in the app: one of your people. Your position goes with the call.
No. Seashell does not call 112 or any public service number. Its help call rings only the contact you chose.
No. It is not a certified distress beacon, and it does not replace one where the rules ask for one. It needs mobile coverage to call.
No. A few centimetres of sea stop the signal. The call goes out when the shell is above the surface and has coverage.
Our target is a 10-hour day on the water: reachable the whole time, about 15 minutes of calls, a few hours of music and one call for help, with a reserve, at sea, with an aged battery (target). Calls non-stop would last about 45 to 90 minutes (estimate). We publish measured hours after the bench tests.
A 30 mm shell holds a small battery, about 96 mAh. Calls use the radio hard, so most of the day goes to being reachable, and the minutes of talk are few.
In its charging cradle, through four small pads in the shell's waist. We have not measured the charge time yet.
A little. The target day already counts cold water and an aged battery.
Wherever there is LTE-M, the mobile network for small devices. At the edge of coverage calls get shorter and the battery runs down faster. We will publish a coverage check before pre-order (plan).
